# Chapter 6 Context: Night of Blue Fire

Source: Local prep file `Chapter 6.txt`, copied from your owned DnD Beyond copy of *Vecna: Eve of Ruin*.

Use this as a DM-facing context file for discussion and prep. It intentionally avoids reproducing the chapter text. Keep the official chapter open for maps, read-aloud text, stat blocks, exact room wording, and appendix monsters.

## Chapter Spine

The party follows the Rod of Seven Parts to Krynn. The rod points them to a dying magical peylon tree, but the actual fifth rod piece has already been stolen and taken to Three Moons Vault. Teremini Nightsedge, an evil elf archmage in Lord Soth's service, is using the rod piece to fuel a lunar ritual that would make the vault's red moonlight curse permanent.

The practical adventure flow is:

1. Arrive on Krynn and investigate the dying peylon tree.
2. Meet Riffel, a kender werewolf and Blue Fire Warden, who knows where the rod piece went.
3. Secure the last ripe peylon fruit from Gazaia, the corrupted dryad guardian.
4. Help Riffel rescue trapped Blue Fire Wardens from a borthak at Bittergrass Fen.
5. Learn the vault situation from Argentia and receive Riffel's guide support.
6. Infiltrate Three Moons Vault through a secret route.
7. Rescue Valendar, leader of the Blue Fire Wardens.
8. Learn how the moonlight mirrors and lunar crystals can disrupt the ritual.
9. Find enough mirrors and the correct lunar-crystal pairings.
10. Stop Teremini's ritual, seize the fifth rod piece, survive the fallout, and return to Sigil.

## Campaign Function

- Starting level: 15.
- Advancement: the party levels after retrieving the fifth rod piece.
- Main artifact objective: fifth piece of the Rod of Seven Parts.
- Chapter villain: Teremini Nightsedge.
- Larger threat presence: Lord Soth is felt through servants, undead, intimidation, and a scrying sphere.
- Complication: Orinix, a lunar dragon, arrives after the ritual is disrupted and attacks broadly, prioritizing Teremini.

## What Must Be Clear at the Table

The party needs to understand:

- The rod's guidance is not wrong, but it is outdated/warped.
- The tree once held the rod piece; Soth's forces stole it recently.
- The Blue Fire Wardens are benevolent lycanthropes, not default enemies.
- Teremini's red moonlight is especially dangerous to werewolves.
- Valendar is both a rescue target and a key clue source.
- The ritual puzzle is not "destroy random things"; it is about moonlight color balance.
- The final moonlight solution is:
  - Red moonlight onto the white crystal.
  - White moonlight onto the black crystal.
  - Black moonlight onto the red crystal.

If any of these do not land, the dungeon can turn into a blind room-clear. Reinforce them through Riffel, Argentia, Valendar, Casivus, Teremini's logbook, the orrery, and visible tower color theming.

## Important Secrets

These matter for the chapter's Power of Secrets rules.

- Gazaia's secret: she hid during the attack on the peylon tree and feels responsible for failing to protect it.
- Valendar's secret: he led the Blue Fire Warden attack without enough scouting or planning, and he blames himself for the defeat.

Good ways to surface them:

- Let Gazaia show grief before hostility. The party should have a chance to comfort, confront, or bargain with her.
- Let Valendar be embarrassed and evasive when he discusses the failed assault. A direct, compassionate question should draw it out.

## Key NPCs

### Riffel

- Kender werewolf, Blue Fire Warden, neutral good.
- First ally and local guide.
- Wants peylon fruit to distract the borthak and save trapped wardens.
- Can lead the party to the secret vault entrance.
- Protected by a one-time Warden rite that also shields nearby allies from the vault's forced-transformation effect for 12 hours.

Table use:

- Make Riffel urgent but not reckless.
- He should pull the party toward action, not solve the vault.
- His small size matters in the fen scene because he can squeeze through the temple-door gap.

### Gazaia

- Deadbark dryad tied to the dying peylon tree.
- Grieving, ashamed, angry, and corrupted by the tree's decay.
- Has the last ripe peylon fruit.
- Wants a magic item in exchange.
- Attacks if refused and not respected, or if the fruit is stolen.

Table use:

- This scene works best as tense negotiation, not automatic combat.
- Her price can feel steep. Consider foreshadowing that any magic item will do, even a low-utility item.
- If the party treats her pain seriously, reward that with the secret.

### Rosintar

- Evil treant in the peylon tree with two giant spiders.
- Hates intruders.
- Surrenders when badly wounded.
- Knows about Gazaia and saw a blue-cloaked small intruder, Riffel.

Table use:

- This is the chapter's first fight, but it can become an information scene if the party accepts surrender.
- Rosintar should feel like a symptom of the tree's corruption.

### Argentia Skywright

- Human werewolf and Blue Fire Warden spokesperson after the fen rescue.
- Explains the Night of Blue Fire and Teremini's red moonlight trap.
- Gives the party a Spell Scroll of Moonbeam.
- Leads the rite that protects Riffel and nearby allies for 12 hours.

Table use:

- She should give the party the mission brief before the vault.
- Make her practical: she cannot storm the vault again, but she can equip and inform the party.

### Valendar

- Werewolf leader of the Blue Fire Wardens, chaotic good.
- Imprisoned in V7 and forced into a violent hybrid state.
- Key source for the vault layout, lunariums, crystals, and mirror strategy.
- Wants to get back to the wardens once rescued.

Table use:

- The party may need to subdue or de-escalate him before he can help.
- The book calls out bringing him near death as the sure way to revert him; you can decide whether removing/covering the red mirror also allows a less brutal recovery.
- His guilt is a good roleplay hook after the immediate danger passes.

### Casivus

- Guardian naga in the lunar shrine, not aligned with Teremini.
- Initially indifferent.
- Can allow the party to take a white moonlight mirror.
- Knows the ritual depends on the current moon positions.
- Knows the passphrase for V16: "buried is best".

Table use:

- Casivus is an anti-stall NPC. If the players are stuck, Casivus can aim them at the orrery.
- He does not need to join the party. He works better as a shrine-bound witness.

### Akaazi

- Human necromancer wizard serving Lord Soth/Teremini.
- Runs undead creation in the black tower.
- Has access to the locked stairwell passphrase.
- Dies for the cause.

Table use:

- Akaazi is the dungeon's necromantic lieutenant and can anchor the black tower's horror tone.

### Teremini Nightsedge

- Lawful evil elf archmage.
- Oversees Three Moons Vault for Lord Soth.
- Formerly rejected by the ancient moon wardens; this fuels her vendetta.
- Wants permanent red moonlight over the vault and revenge against the Blue Fire Wardens.
- Uses the fifth rod piece as ritual fuel.

Table use:

- She is not just guarding treasure; she is in the middle of a revenge ritual.
- Have her taunt or speak through minions if the party trips alarms.
- Once the ritual fails, she should snap from controlled ritualist to furious combatant.

### Orinix

- Adult lunar dragon.
- Taught Teremini the ritual.
- Arrives through a portal after the ritual is disrupted.
- Attacks everyone nearby, focusing especially on Teremini.

Table use:

- Orinix is a complication, not necessarily a final boss the party must defeat.
- Use the dragon to turn the finale into chaos and motion rather than a static archmage fight.

## Scene 1: Arrival and Peylon Tree

Purpose:

- Establish Krynn's moons and war-torn mood.
- Show that the rod points to the tree but the piece is gone.
- Introduce the chapter's nature-corruption theme.
- Lead to Riffel and the Blue Fire Wardens.

Important facts:

- The tree grew huge because the rod piece had empowered it.
- The rod piece was recently removed by Soth's forces.
- The tree and Gazaia deteriorated after the theft.
- Riffel is searching for the last fruit because the borthak loves peylon fruit.

Locations:

- P1: Rotted roots and main entrance; tracks lead inward.
- P2: Hollow; rod points to a boulder hiding the grotto access.
- P3: Ledge; Rosintar and giant spiders ambush or confront.
- P4: Grotto; Riffel and Gazaia; fruit negotiation.

Checks and mechanics to remember:

- Tracks around tree: Perception or Survival DC 14.
- Spiders on ledge: Perception DC 17, or passive 15 to notice their attack preparation.
- Riffel can be calmed by mentioning the rod or succeeding on Persuasion DC 13.
- Moving the boulder requires combined Strength 30, or 1 minute of digging to widen the hole.
- Grotto drop is 50 feet.

Potential improvements:

- Give the tree a few sensory beats that show it is not merely dead but violated.
- Let the party see that Gazaia's hostility comes from shame and grief.
- If the party refuses to pay a magic item, consider allowing a hard bargain: oath, future favor, or return of the rod's magic after the vault. Keep the fruit obtainable, because the fen scene depends on it.

## Scene 2: Bittergrass Fen

Purpose:

- Convert Riffel from suspicious stranger to proven ally.
- Show the Wardens as victims and protectors.
- Provide a dynamic rescue encounter before the dungeon.
- Deliver the vault briefing through Argentia.

Encounter shape:

- Borthak attacks a temple door while wardens are trapped inside.
- The party can fight, distract, manipulate terrain, or help evacuate.
- Riffel squeezes through the temple-door gap and helps the wardens get out.
- Once wardens escape, they transform into wolves and flee.

Battlefield tools:

- Ripe peylon fruit can distract the borthak.
- Floating boulders can be pushed or dispelled to fall.
- Mud near the creek is difficult terrain.
- The door has high durability; the borthak will break through if ignored.

DM handling:

- Track the borthak's door damage visibly or narratively so the rescue has tension.
- Let clever terrain use matter more than pure DPR.
- End the encounter when the wardens are safe or the borthak is defeated.
- If the party burns major resources, the vault's 12-hour Warden protection creates an interesting time pressure.

Reward/information:

- Argentia thanks the party.
- She explains Teremini's red moonlight trap and the failed Warden assault.
- She says Valendar may know how to stop the ritual.
- She gives a Spell Scroll of Moonbeam.
- She leads the one-time rite protecting Riffel and nearby allies from forced transformation for 12 hours.

## Scene 3: Three Moons Vault Overview

Purpose:

- Infiltration/rescue mission that becomes a ritual puzzle and boss confrontation.

Party goals:

- Rescue Valendar.
- Stop Teremini's ritual.
- Retrieve the fifth rod piece.

Environmental effects within 1 mile of the vault:

- Forced Transformation: werewolves entering the red moonlight become wolf/hybrid, are poisoned, and cannot willingly change back unless reduced to 10 hp or fewer. Remove Curse suppresses this for 1 hour.
- Reduced Gravity: fall damage is halved and jumps go twice as far.
- The vault's internal locations suppress the broader environmental effects unless a specific room/mirror says otherwise.

Entry:

- Main entrance is obvious and guarded.
- Riffel leads the party to a secret cliff/goat-path entrance at V1.
- Riffel waits outside so he can escort Valendar back to the wardens.

Vault tone:

- Castle-prison built on ancient moon-shrine ruins.
- Three moon towers: white/Solinari, red/Lunitari, black/Nuitari.
- Lord Soth's influence is undead, militarized, punitive, and theatrical.
- Teremini overlays wizardly lunar obsession and personal revenge.

## Vault Alarm Logic

Dungeon level:

- Wraiths in V4 carry horns.
- If they sound a horn, the undead in V8 can join.
- Dungeon horn noise does not carry to upper keep areas.

Keep level:

- Veterans in V19 and V22 have horns.
- V19 can pull up the ladder from V17.
- V22 can summon V18 support, arriving after about 5 minutes.
- V33 functions as a rally point when the keep alarm is raised.

DM handling:

- Decide before running whether the vault is "alerted" after any failed stealth.
- Use alarms to create moving pressure, but avoid turning every room into one giant combat unless the party deliberately goes loud.
- If the party earns stealth, let them bypass some fights.

## Moonlight Mirror Cheat Sheet

Moonlight mirrors are portable ritual tools. They are fragile magical objects and cast colored moonlight unless covered.

White mirrors:

- V15 Lunar Shrine: held by Solinari statue, guarded by Casivus.
- V20 Ruined Shrine: hidden under floor tiles; Investigation DC 12.

Red mirrors:

- V7 Valendar's Cell: ceiling mirror; no special removal effort.
- V30 Red Mirror Stairwell: wall mirror; no special removal effort. Removing it stops the V29 orrery.

Black mirrors:

- V28 Royal Quarters: affixed to Lord Soth's throne; no special removal effort.
- V36 Akaazi's Quarters: suspended over V34; Sleight of Hand DC 15 to detach safely. Failing by 5 or more drops it.

Final ritual pairing:

- Red light -> white crystal.
- White light -> black crystal.
- Black light -> red crystal.

Information sources:

- Valendar explains mirrors and crystals generally.
- Casivus explains that the current lunar positions determine the correct combination and points toward the orrery.
- Teremini's logbook lists mirror locations.
- V29 orrery gives the exact relationship needed to derive the pairings.

Anti-stall options:

- If the V30 mirror is removed before the party studies the orrery, let the orrery freeze in a readable position, or let Casivus interpret what he remembers.
- If the party misses Valendar, let the logbook and Casivus provide enough puzzle context.
- If the party misses Casivus, let Valendar explicitly say the red tower contains Teremini's moon-position device.

## Upper Level and Finale

Upper-level features:

- Each lunarium has colored glass tied to one moon.
- Lunar crystals are on daises and protected by solid moonlight barriers while Teremini concentrates.
- Moonbridges and the moondisk are solid moonlight.
- Creatures not in true form can be poisoned by touching solid moonlight.
- Solid moonlight blocks physical passage, teleportation, and ethereal travel.
- Sunburst suppresses solid moonlight for 10 minutes.
- Antimagic Field does not negate the ritual because the rod piece powers it.

Areas:

- U1 White Lunarium: white crystal; barrier trap can blind for 24 hours on a failed Con save.
- U2 Red Lunarium: deathwolf guard.
- U3 Black Lunarium: two black rose bearers.
- U4 Moonbridges: solid moonlight bridges to moondisk.
- U5 Moondisk: Teremini ritual site and fifth rod piece.

Disrupting the ritual:

- Place the right mirror colors on the right crystals, or remove a lunar crystal after barriers fall/conditions allow.
- Once disrupted, solid moonlight surfaces disappear.
- Creatures on the moondisk or bridges fall 100 feet, with reduced gravity halving damage if the environmental effect applies.
- Portal to Lunitari opens and Orinix arrives.
- Teremini grabs/pockets the rod piece and fights.

Finale handling:

- Warn players through visual cues that solid moonlight is unstable before it vanishes.
- Let characters make urgent choices: grab a crystal, leap, fly, teleport where possible, save an ally, seize the rod, avoid Orinix.
- Orinix focusing Teremini gives the party a chance to claim the rod without needing to defeat both archmage and dragon.

## Clue Chain

Use this to keep the adventure moving if a scene goes sideways.

Rod points to dying tree:

- The tree is the old location, not the current location.
- Evidence: missing magical heart, decay, Gazaia's memory, Riffel's explanation.

Tree points to vault:

- Riffel knows the artifact was taken to Three Moons Vault.
- Gazaia can confirm Soth's soldiers stole it.

Vault points to Valendar:

- Argentia says Valendar knows the most.
- Riffel wants to rescue him.

Valendar points to mirrors and crystals:

- He knows the ritual depends on lunar crystals and magical balance.
- He suggests collecting one mirror of each color.

Casivus/logbook/orerry point to exact solution:

- Casivus explains that the current moon positions matter and directs the party to V29.
- Logbook tells where mirrors are.
- Orrery reveals the exact pairings.

## Likely Table Problems and Fixes

Problem: The party kills Gazaia before learning her secret.

- Fix: Put the fruit on her body as written, but let signs in the grotto reveal her shame. A successful post-fight Nature/Insight/Religion check can still uncover the emotional truth if you want the secret available.

Problem: The party refuses to give up a magic item.

- Fix: Let Gazaia accept a minor permanent magic item, a charged item, or a sworn future restitution. If they try to bully her, combat is fair.

Problem: The borthak becomes a static HP grind.

- Fix: Run it as a rescue clock. Put wardens, door damage, fruit, floating boulders, and mud in play every round.

Problem: The party wants the Blue Fire Wardens to join the vault assault.

- Fix: The red moonlight curse makes them liabilities inside the aura. Their best contribution is the warding rite, intelligence, and extraction of Valendar.

Problem: The players miss Valendar.

- Fix: Use his howls, prisoner records, Guerthel's key, or Riffel's urgency to point them toward V7.

Problem: The mirror puzzle stalls.

- Fix: Make each clue source partial but stackable. If they have any two of Valendar, Casivus, logbook, or orrery, they should be able to solve it.

Problem: They remove the V30 red mirror before reading the orrery.

- Fix: Let the orrery preserve its last position, or let a successful Arcana check reconstruct the reading from the frozen mechanism.

Problem: The party tries to bypass the ritual with teleportation.

- Fix: Solid moonlight blocks teleportation through it. Let teleportation still be useful for movement around non-blocked spaces.

Problem: Orinix makes the finale feel unwinnable.

- Fix: Treat Orinix as environmental chaos and pressure. It prioritizes Teremini, giving players windows to escape, grab the rod, or exploit the conflict.

Prep focus:

- Draw a one-page mirror tracker.
- Decide how long Teremini's ritual pressure feels in-world.

## Party-Specific Questions To Answer

These will help tune the chapter to your table:

1. Who is in the party at level 15: classes, subclasses, species, major magic items, and standout spells?
2. Does anyone have lycanthropy, moon, nature, undead, dragon, or Krynn/Dragonlance ties?
3. How have they used the Rod of Seven Parts so far, and who usually carries it?
4. How tactical is the group: do they enjoy infiltration puzzles, or will they default to direct combat?
5. How lethal do you want this chapter to feel?
6. Are you running 2014 rules, 2024 rules, or a hybrid?
7. Do you want Lord Soth to be a distant menace here or an active presence through the scrying sphere?
8. Do you want Orinix to be a fight, a hazard, a bargaining opportunity, or an escape pressure?
9. Are you using a VTT, printed maps, theater of the mind, or a mix?
10. How much do your players remember about Lord Soth, Krynn, or Dragonlance lore?
