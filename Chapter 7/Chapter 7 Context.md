# Chapter 7 Context: Tomb of Wayward Souls

## Chapter Spine

The party follows the sixth rod piece to Oerth, on the Isle of Serpents. They arrive during a kraken attack against Laysa Matulin's archaeological expedition, then learn that Acererak built a deathtrap tomb on the island after stealing treasures from Laysa's ancestors.

The practical flow is:

1. Arrive through the Sigil portal at the lagoon.
2. Decide whether and how to save the archaeologists from the wounded kraken.
3. Meet Laysa, Talo, Vogren, and Marian at camp.
4. Learn that the rod piece is probably inside the Tomb of Wayward Souls.
5. Enter the tomb, bypassing false entrances, hidden doors, mirages, and lethal traps.
6. Recover Laysa's ancestral treasures if the party chooses.
7. Learn the crypt-door combination from the library or laboratory.
8. Confront Rerak, Acererak's resentful false lich simulacrum.
9. Claim the sixth rod piece from the vault.
10. Return to camp, resolve Laysa/Rerak fallout, and return to Sigil.

## Campaign Function

- Starting level: 16.
- Advancement: the party levels after retrieving the sixth rod piece.
- Main artifact objective: sixth piece of the Rod of Seven Parts.
- Main chapter villain/guardian: Rerak, an empowered simulacrum of Acererak.
- Tone: classic Acererak tomb logic: bait, false routes, magical traps, cruel jokes, and clues hidden inside danger.
- Main complication: the tomb is not just treasure storage. Rerak harvests dead adventurers' souls for Acererak.

## What Must Be Clear at the Table

The party should understand:

- The rod points to the Isle of Serpents because the sixth piece is in Acererak's tomb.
- Laysa is not a quest giver for the rod, but her ancestral treasure hunt aligns with the party's goal.
- The archaeologists know the entrance area only; they cannot map or solve the tomb for the party.
- The false entrances are meant to teach caution before the true tomb opens up.
- Hidden doors are a major tomb language. Touch, truesight, and `Dispel Magic` matter.
- The tomb walls resist shape-changing magic, and planar travel fails inside the complex.
- The mirages are not random illusions; they point toward Rerak's trapped, resentful mind.
- The final confrontation can be solved socially if the party realizes Rerak hates his imprisonment.
- If someone dies in the tomb, their soul is on a 24-hour clock before Acererak claims it forever.

## Important Secrets

These matter for the Power of Secrets rules.

- Marian's secret: she once admired Acererak and considered necromancy before rejecting that path.
- Rerak's secret: he never truly wanted to serve Acererak and has been miserable and lonely for centuries.

Good ways to surface them:

- Let Marian be useful but visibly uncomfortable when discussing Acererak's brilliance.
- Let the spirit lights, mirages, and Rerak's own commands hint that the tomb guardian wants release, not victory.
- Rerak's secret should be available only if the party engages with his imprisonment, not if they treat him as a silent loot barrier.

## Key NPCs

### Laysa Matulin

- Pirate-turned-archaeologist, descended from the island's original inhabitants.
- Wants to reclaim six stolen ancestral treasures from the tomb.
- Gives the party a rough tomb-complex map and can lead them to the entrance.
- Pays for returned ancestral treasures.

Table use:

- Make her direct, capable, and emotionally invested.
- She should care about the ancestral items at least as much as the party cares about the rod.

### Talo Yieria and Vogren Starcloak

- Priests of Celestian and healers at the archaeological camp.
- Emphasize reciprocity with the island's spirits: leave offerings, show respect, do not only take.
- Can heal the party if they return to camp.

Table use:

- Use them to introduce the island's moral logic before the tomb rewards players who give offerings.
- Their healing support is useful, but do not let it erase tomb pressure entirely.

### Marian Xavere

- Mage and arcane specialist fascinated by Acererak.
- Can explain the archlich's reputation and the danger of tomb magic.
- Can lend a Lantern of Revealing, and might lend a Gem of Seeing with persuasion.
- Holds one of the chapter secrets.

Table use:

- She is a good source of lore, but she should underestimate the tomb's personal cruelty.
- If Zagdreth or another caster engages her, lean into technical wizard curiosity and moral discomfort.

### Rerak

- False lich simulacrum of Acererak.
- Bound to the rod piece and unable to leave while it remains in the tomb.
- Harvests dead adventurers' souls through gems in his eye sockets.
- Loyal by design, resentful by lived experience.
- Can be defeated, or turned against Acererak after three successful persuasive breakthroughs during combat.

Table use:

- Rerak should feel like a boss, prisoner, and warning sign all at once.
- He should command the party to flee in a way that sounds angry on the surface and miserable underneath.
- If the party learned about Acererak's disdain for simulacra in T23, reward that with advantage on the social route.

## Core Tomb Rules

- Walls are immune to `Passwall`, `Stone Shape`, and similar magic.
- Planar movement such as `Plane Shift`, `Banishment`, `Etherealness`, and similar effects consumes resources but fails.
- Hidden doors are illusion-concealed but physically real. Touch and truesight reveal them; `Dispel Magic` removes concealment.
- Green flames brightly light the tomb and cannot be extinguished.
- Rerak resets triggered traps over time.
- Mirages appear in T9, T13, T21, and T24.

## Tomb Route Notes

The true entrance path starts at T3, but the party may learn important behavior from T1 and T2:

- T1 and T2 are false entrances with dangerous traps.
- T2 gives the clue that the blue devil face in T4 needs magic.
- T3 hides the route to T5 under the spike pit.
- T4 can either open the route to T6 by feeding spell slots, or teleport creatures to T14 through the mouth.
- T5's cask puzzle is the clean route to T8.
- T8 is the archway hub and can send careless explorers into void closets or backtracking loops.
- T19 and T23 both reveal the T25 crypt-door combination.
- T26 is the Rerak confrontation.
- T27 contains the sixth rod piece and the final ancestral treasure.

## Party-Specific Tuning

The party is level 16 and already showed that a major solo creature can disappear in one turn. Zagdreth one-shotting Orinix is the signal: do not rely on a single exposed boss with normal hit points to carry the finale.

Recommended global adjustments:

- Treat combat as objective pressure, not only HP depletion.
- Use max HP for major monsters by default.
- Give important solos battlefield support, wards, minions, or phase objectives.
- Let high passive Investigation and `True Seeing` find clues quickly, but still require choices under pressure.
- Keep the tomb's built-in anti-bypass rules visible: walls resist shape-changing magic, and planar travel fails.
- Do not make every trap harder. The tomb already punishes recklessness; focus upgrades on encounters that would otherwise collapse.

Kraken attack:

- Run it as a rescue clock. Each round the kraken threatens one or more archaeologists unless stopped, moved, blinded, restrained, or driven off.
- Consider raising the wounded kraken from 250 hp to 350-400 hp if you want it to survive past the opening nova.
- Give it a goal other than deathmatch: grab prey, crush boats/camp supplies, then retreat if bloodied.

Tomb pressure:

- Rests should have consequences. Rerak can reset traps, move false treasure, seed more mirage warnings, or advance the 24-hour soul-transfer clock if anyone died.
- Returning to camp for healing is fine, but repeated full resets should make the tomb feel like it is learning.
- For this party, clues should be easy to obtain. The challenge should be sequencing, risk, and deciding what to take.

Rerak adjustment options:

- Use max HP, and consider adding 100-150 temporary hit points from soul energy.
- Give Rerak legendary resistance if his stat block does not already have enough protection.
- Add two soul-gem wards in his eye sockets. While at least one ward remains, Rerak has advantage on saves against spells and resistance to damage from spells. Each ward can be targeted as an object or shattered by one successful social breakthrough.
- When a ward breaks, release trapped souls as a room pulse: necrotic damage, forced movement, fear, or a wave of grasping hands. Keep it short and obvious.
- Let false treasures on the pedestals pulse or animate as lair actions if the fight needs another objective.
- Rerak should not stand still in an open room waiting to be erased. He knows the tomb, uses cover/spacing, and pressures concentration.

Use only the adjustments you need. The cleanest upgrade is: max HP, two soul-gem wards, and lair-action pressure from the crypt.

## Likely Table Problems And Fixes

Problem: The players ignore the archaeologists during the kraken attack.

- Fix: Let the consequences stand, but keep Laysa, Talo, Vogren, and Marian alive as written. The camp is colder, and Laysa's trust is harder earned.

Problem: `True Seeing` reveals many hidden doors and mirages.

- Fix: Let it work. The cost is a high-level resource, and this party is built for arcane problem solving. Shift tension to what they do with the information.

Problem: The party brute-forces traps with healing.

- Fix: Use clocks and compounding effects. Soul harvesting, trap resets, and Rerak's awareness matter more than raw damage.

Problem: Rerak gets nuked before his roleplay lands.

- Fix: Start his dialogue before initiative, then use soul-gem wards to prevent a one-turn deletion. Each ward can carry one piece of his emotional truth.

Problem: The party misses the T23 notes.

- Fix: Let T9 spirit lights, Rerak's miserable commands, or Marian's Acererak lore provide partial social leverage.

Problem: The party wants to keep all ancestral treasures.

- Fix: That is a real choice. Laysa pays well, but some items are powerful. Tie the moral weight back to the island's reciprocity theme.
